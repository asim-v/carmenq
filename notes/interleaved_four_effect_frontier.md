At support weight \(\lambda=0.6\), it gives

\[
L_{3\mathrm E}(0.6)=0.755705934586018\ldots .
\]

The four-effect construction below instead gives

\[
\boxed{

\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{L_{4\mathrm E}(0.6)=0.765898815264694\ldots .}

\(1.2\times10^{-15}\).  Thus the value is a legal complete streamed strategy
after local Pauli completion.

## 2. Symmetric four-effect geometry

Let

\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\0&1-p\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \frac12\leq p\leq1,
\]

and put

\[
v_s=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
v_\ell=(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

The first binary group consists of the rank-one effects generated by

\[
g_s=E^{1/2}v_s,
\qquad
g_\ell=E^{1/2}v_\ell.
\]

Their outer products sum to \(E\).  The second group is the bit-flip image,

\[
g_2=Xg_\ell,
\qquad
g_3=Xg_s,
\]

whose outer products sum to \(I-E\).  Hence all four effects form a qubit
POVM exactly.

Use the four pure middle-cut states

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\psi_s\rangle&=(\sqrt{u_s},\sqrt{1-u_s})^{\mathsf T},\\
|\psi_\ell\rangle&=(\sqrt{u_\ell},-\sqrt{1-u_\ell})^{\mathsf T},\\
|\psi_2\rangle&=X|\psi_\ell\rangle,\\
|\psi_3\rangle&=X|\psi_s\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\]

Define

\[
q_j=(1-p)+(2p-1)u_j,
\qquad
c_j=\sqrt{q_j}+\sqrt{1-q_j},
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_s&=\left(
\sqrt p\sin\theta\sqrt{u_s}
+\sqrt{1-p}\cos\theta\sqrt{1-u_s}
\right)^2,\\
d_\ell&=\left(
\sqrt p\cos\theta\sqrt{u_\ell}
+\sqrt{1-p}\sin\theta\sqrt{1-u_\ell}
\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For fixed \((p,\theta,u_s,u_\ell)\), all four prefix priors are optimised by
the Perron eigenvector of

\[
M_{4\mathrm E}(\lambda)
=\lambda\operatorname{diag}(d_s,d_\ell)
+\frac{1-\lambda}{4}
\begin{pmatrix}c_s\\c_\ell\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}c_s&c_\ell\end{pmatrix}.
\]

If its normalised nonnegative top eigenvector is \((w_s,w_\ell)\), the four
priors are

\[
(a_s,a_\ell,a_2,a_3)
=\left(\frac{w_s^2}{2},\frac{w_\ell^2}{2},
       \frac{w_\ell^2}{2},\frac{w_s^2}{2}\right).
\]

The exact support within the family is therefore the four-scalar maximum

\[
\boxed{
L_{4\mathrm E}(\lambda)
=\max_{p,\theta,u_s,u_\ell}
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(M_{4\mathrm E}(\lambda)\right).}
\]

No variational prior variables remain.

## 3. Explicit bond-two realisation

The first two slots prepare the four subnormalised pure states above while
emitting computational records of \(x_1,x_2\).  Their two-site tensor has
Schmidt rank at most two because the first paired physical index has only the
two occupied sectors \((B_1,x_1)=(0,0),(1,1)\).

At slot three, for label \(i=2x_3+j\), use

\[
T_3|x_3\rangle|\phi\rangle
=|x_3\rangle_{B_3}|j\rangle_M\langle g_i|\phi\rangle .
\]

The emitted carrier stores \(x_3\), while the persistent qubit stores the
decision \(j\).  The four pairs \((x_3,j)\) are orthogonal, and POVM
completeness gives the local row-isometry identity.  This orthogonal output
assignment is essential: storing \(j\) twice instead would leave an avoidable
which-effect record and fail to saturate RETURN.

At slot four use

\[
T_{4,x_4}=2^{-1/2}|x_4\rangle_{B_4}\otimes X^{x_4}_M.
\]

The terminal POVM is

\[
E_{00}=|0\rangle\!\langle0|,
\qquad
E_{01}=|1\rangle\!\langle1|,
\qquad
E_{10}=E_{11}=0.
\]

AUDIT succeeds precisely through the four effects \(|g_i\rangle\langle g_i|\).
Every input bit is also recorded in a distinct emitted-carrier basis state,
so the sixteen Choi columns are orthogonal.  Computational pinching is thus
an equality and gives the Hellinger RETURN term in \(M_{4\mathrm E}\).

## 5. Analytic endpoint separation from 3E

Write \(\varepsilon=1-\lambda\).  A simple subfamily already proves the new
asymptotic improvement.  Set

\[
p=1-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{16},\qquad
\theta=0,\qquad u_s=\frac12,\qquad u_\ell=1,
\]

and retain the exact Perron optimisation of the priors.  Direct expansion of
the two-by-two eigenvalue gives

\[
L_{4\mathrm E}(1-\varepsilon)
\geq
1-\frac34\varepsilon
+\frac3{16}\varepsilon^2
+\frac9{32}\varepsilon^3
+O(\varepsilon^4).
\]

The optimised three-effect family instead has

\[
L_{3\mathrm E}(1-\varepsilon)
=1-\frac34\varepsilon+\frac18\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]

Therefore 3E is analytically excluded as the full frontier in a punctured
neighbourhood of \(\lambda=1\), independently of floating-point optimisation.

## 6. Exact reduction of the binary-line sector

The construction belongs to a larger sector that can be reduced exactly.
Let \(G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3\succeq0\) sum to \(I_2\), put
\(E=G_0+G_1\), and let four pure prefix states with priors \(a_i\) interrogate
the four correct effects.  After optimising the priors, the support is the top
eigenvalue of

\[
M=\lambda\operatorname{diag}(d_i)
+\frac{1-\lambda}{8}cc^{\mathsf T},
\]

where

\[
d_i=\langle\psi_i|G_i|\psi_i\rangle,
\]

and \(c_i=\sqrt{q_i}+\sqrt{1-q_i}\), with \(q_i\) the appropriate coarse
probability for \(E\) or \(I-E\).

For a proposed upper value \(L>\lambda\max_i d_i\), the matrix determinant
lemma gives the equivalent secular condition

\[
\frac{1-\lambda}{8}
\sum_i\frac{c_i^2}{L-\lambda d_i}\leq1.
\]

Fix a coarse effect \(E\).  For a binary split \((G,E-G)\), the left side is
a convex function of \(G\in[0,E]\): for every fixed state, the reciprocal of
the positive affine denominator is convex and increasing, and the supremum
over states preserves convexity.  Its maximum is therefore attained at an
extreme point of the operator interval.  Those extreme points are

\[
G=E^{1/2}PE^{1/2},
\]

where \(P\) is a projection on the support of \(E\).  In dimension two, each
binary group consequently has only two topologies:

1. an endpoint split \((E,0)\); or
2. two congruence rank-one effects generated by \((P,I-P)\).

For fixed coarse expectation, the ratio increases with the correct-effect
expectation.  A linear functional on a qubit Bloch slice is maximised at a
pure state, so mixed prefix states are unnecessary.  Diagonalising \(E\) then
leaves only its two eigenvalues, one projector angle for each rank split, and
one scalar state coordinate per effect.  Thus the entire binary-line sector
is exactly the maximum over four finite scalar topologies:

\[
(\text{endpoint},\text{endpoint}),\quad
(\text{endpoint},\text{rank}),\quad
(\text{rank},\text{endpoint}),\quad
(\text{rank},\text{rank}).
\]

Broad differential evolution, deterministic continuation, and unrestricted
complex Choi-MPS optimisation identify 3E and symmetric 4E as the exposed
branches on the tested support grid.  That numerical last sentence is not a
replacement for interval certification of every remaining scalar maximum.

## 7. Two-block reduction and what remains before “complete frontier” is justified

The arbitrary adaptive problem is exactly one normalised bond-two Choi-MPS
maximum by the local Weyl-completion theorem.  The four-effect point is a
feasible MPS and is reproduced by unrestricted complex optimisation.  The
pinched cq suffix relaxation also returns the same value at \(\lambda=0.6\).

The four-slot problem is upper-bounded by a two-block relaxation obtained by
removing the internal cuts within \((x_1,x_2)\) and \((x_3,x_4)\) while
retaining the central qubit.  Unrestricted complex optimisation of that
larger class reproduces 3E/4E at the tested exposed points.  Moreover, if its
terminal POVM has two projective outcomes, the model reduces *exactly* to the
binary-line scalar topologies above.  The proof is in
`notes/two_block_projective_frontier.md`.

At \(\lambda=0.6\), the binary-projective sector is no longer merely a
variational match.  A hierarchical SCIP cover of all four scalar topologies
gives

\[
0.765898815264694\ldots
\leq\beta_{\rm projective}(0.6)
\leq0.76591.
\]

The 312-leaf cover has maximum scaled secular dual
\(0.9999905164\), and its interval width is
\(1.1185\times10^{-5}\).  The validator and scope statement are in
`scratch/d2_frontier/validate_projective_cover.py` and
`notes/two_block_projective_frontier.md`.  This is a global numerical SCIP
certificate at the recorded tolerances, not an interval-arithmetic proof.

The terminal discrimination problem also has an exact geometric
classification.  Four subnormalised qubit states
\(\tau_s=(p_sI+r_s\cdot\sigma)/2\) obey

\[
P_{\rm guess}
=\min_y\max_s\left(p_s+\lVert y-r_s\rVert_2\right).
\]

Two active constraints give a binary projective POVM; three or four active
constraints are exactly the genuine nonprojective sectors.  The unrestricted
complex 4E checkpoint is strictly two-active: its two inactive constraints
have slacks above \(0.781\).  See
`notes/terminal_qubit_discrimination_geometry.md`.

The former missing converse was a terminal-projectivity lemma.  That lemma is
still not known and is no longer assumed.  At \(\lambda=0.6\), the
three-active and four-active Helstrom sectors have instead been covered
directly.  Combining those covers with the projective certificate gives

\[
0.7658988152646944
\leq\beta_{2\mathrm b}(0.6)
\leq0.76662.
\]

The upper endpoint is a complete finite solver-conditional numerical
enclosure: analytic arity reduction and Helstrom identities are combined with
a 12,008-leaf CLARABEL ternary cover and a spatial SCIP dual for the fully
active region.  It is not an interval-arithmetic proof, and it does not yet
establish equality with 4E.  The complete sector split, exact comparison
identities, deletion argument, artifacts, and epistemic boundary are recorded
in `notes/interleaved_interior_frontier_l060.md`.

Thus the open problem has moved again.  At this support direction, the task is
to shrink or formally validate a \(7.22\times10^{-4}\) numerical enclosure;
globally, the support curve for other values of \(\lambda\) still requires
corresponding sector covers or a uniform analytic converse.  The exact
common-instrument Choi programme remains useful as the starting point in
`notes/two_block_choi_program.md`.

The curated independent verifier is
`scripts/verify_four_effect_interleaved_candidate.py`; exploratory topology,
continuation, cq-relaxation, and interval scripts are documented under
`scratch/d2_frontier/`.
