# Operator-basis criterion for a common qubit instrument

## Literature boundary

The underlying uniqueness statement is standard quantum process tomography,
not a new theorem. Four linearly independent qubit input states determine a
linear process completely; this is stated explicitly by Ziman, Plesch, and
Bužek in [Reconstruction of superoperators from incomplete
measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406088). Conversion between
superoperator and Choi/process-matrix representations is also standard; see
Nambu and Nakamura, [On the Matrix Representation of Quantum
Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0504091). More generally, the
existence of completely positive transformations between statistical models
belongs to quantum comparison theory and statistical morphisms; see Buscemi,
[Comparison of quantum statistical
models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3794).

The contribution here is narrower: package the basis reconstruction as an
exact compatibility test for a *flagged instrument*, expose a
determinant-scaled polynomial matrix inequality suitable for the interior
global optimization, and integrate both into the reproducible CARMEN-Q API.
No priority claim is made for the linear-algebra identity itself.

## Exact criterion

Let \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_3\) be four linearly independent Hermitian qubit
operators and let \(\sigma_{zy}\) be proposed conditioned outputs. Write their
Pauli coefficients as

\[
 R_{z\mu}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_z\sigma_\mu),
 \qquad
 Q^{(y)}_{z\nu}=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{zy}\sigma_\nu).
\]

Since \(R\) is invertible, the unique Hermiticity-preserving map sending every
\(\rho_z\) to \(\sigma_{zy}\) has Pauli-transfer matrix

\[
 L_y=Q_y^{\mathsf T}(R^{\mathsf T})^{-1}.
\]

With the input-major convention used by the package, its Choi matrix is

\[
 J_y=\frac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}
 (L_y)_{\nu\mu}\,\sigma_\mu^{\mathsf T}\otimes\sigma_\nu.
\]

Consequently the proposed family is generated by one quantum instrument if
and only if

\[
 J_y\succeq0\quad\text{for every }y,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\rm out}\sum_yJ_y=I.
\]

Necessity follows from Choi positivity. For sufficiency, the matrices \(J_y\)
define completely positive maps. The partial-trace identity makes their sum
trace preserving, and construction guarantees all sixteen interpolation
equalities. Uniqueness follows because the four inputs span the Hermitian
operator space.

## Polynomial numerator

Set \(\delta=\det R\). The inverse can be removed:

\[
 N_y=Q_y^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{adj}(R^{\mathsf T}),
 \qquad
 K_y=\frac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}
 (N_y)_{\nu\mu}\,\sigma_\mu^{\mathsf T}\otimes\sigma_\nu.
\]

Then \(K_y=\delta J_y\). On either determinant-sign branch, complete
positivity is the polynomial matrix inequality

\[
 \operatorname{sign}(\delta)K_y\succeq0.
\]

Every entry of \(K_y\) is degree four in the joint input/output coordinates:
degree three from the adjugate and degree one from \(Q_y\). This is the exact
replacement for an adaptive list of trace-norm contractions in the
nondegenerate interior. It can be enforced by a matrix-valued moment
localizer, by principal-minor inequalities, or by spatial eigenvector cuts.

The determinant scaling also makes the numerical limitation explicit. Near
\(\delta=0\), the reconstructed channel is ill-conditioned and numerator
positivity alone loses quantitative force. The singular stratum must be
handled separately as a lower-dimensional operator-system extension problem;
the fixed-input Choi projection already implemented in the package remains
the correct general test there.

That separate treatment is now explicit. A maximum-entry-normalized left-null
vector gives four compact coefficient charts, and a complete adaptive cover
excludes the singular stratum at score \(0.758\) for the fixed interior
benchmark. The construction and its solver-conditional status are documented
in `singular_common_instrument_stratum.md`.

## Planar determinant-sign symmetry

For the fixed terminal problem, the three nonzero effects lie in the \(XY\)
Bloch plane and obey

\[
 E_s=X E_s^* X.
\]

This gives an exact orientation-reversing symmetry. Given input states and an
instrument, define

\[
 \rho_z'=\rho_z^*,\qquad
 J_y'=(I\otimes X)J_y^*(I\otimes X).
\]

If \(K_{y\alpha}\) are Kraus operators for the original instrument, then
\(XK_{y\alpha}^*\) are Kraus operators for the transformed one. Complete
positivity and trace preservation are therefore retained. Its conditioned
outputs are \(X\sigma_{zy}^*X\), so

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(E_sX\sigma_{zy}^*X)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(E_s\sigma_{zy}).
\]

Every path probability, the Helstrom score, and the Hellinger return term are
unchanged. Complex conjugation reflects the input Bloch \(Y\) coordinate, so
\(\det R\mapsto-\det R\). Consequently the positive and negative
nondegenerate determinant branches are isometric for this planar frontier;
only one sign and the singular stratum require independent treatment.

A constrained physical seesaw inside the benchmark prior box produced a
common-instrument point of score \(0.7228813076\) with
\(\det R=0.0065750928\). The symmetry generates a negative-determinant partner
with exactly the same score and determinant of the opposite sign. This
establishes non-emptiness of both branches; it is a lower-bound construction,
not an upper certificate.

## Numerical consequence

At the worst fully vectorial Fourier-relaxation point, the input determinant
is \(0.0084665038\) with condition number \(3.55\). The unique interpolating
maps reproduce the proposed outputs to \(5.2\times10^{-17}\) and are trace
preserving in sum to \(1.8\times10^{-16}\), but their four minimum Choi
eigenvalues are approximately

\[
 (-0.206,-0.218,-0.271,-0.275).
\]

Thus the remaining high relaxed point is decisively non-CP and not a
near-singular reconstruction artifact. An exact degree-four Cholesky
formulation accepts physical seeds in both determinant sectors, but its SCIP
root relaxation remains around \(0.7634\), above the target \(0.758\). The
criterion is mathematically complete away from \(\delta=0\); exploiting it
requires a stronger matrix localizer or verified spatial subdivision, not
more unassisted pairwise trace-norm cuts.

A first verified spatial subdivision now uses the scalar shadow of this
criterion.  Positivity of each pulled-back terminal effect is converted by
Cramer's rule into a linear inequality valid over a complete sign-definite
input box.  Exhaustive multi-affine determinant bounds remove a false
singular-stratum diagnosis in all twenty leading cells, but the resulting
1,000-node pilot remains above target.  The proposition, numerical audit, and
reason the next cut must retain matrix-valued Choi positivity are documented
in `determinant_scaled_common_povm_witnesses.md`.

## Implementation

`reconstruct_common_instrument_from_basis` returns the unique transfer and
Choi matrices, determinant, condition number, signed Choi numerators, output
residual, trace-preservation residual, and minimum Choi eigenvalues. It has no
solver dependency. Tests cover a physical instrument, positive conditioned
outputs that reconstruct to a non-CP map, the determinant identity, and the
singular-input rejection path.
