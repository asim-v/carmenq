# Determinant-scaled Ando witnesses for a common planar instrument

## Status

This note records a stronger, falsifiable step in the interior-frontier
programme at support weight $\lambda=0.55$. It does not claim that a common
instrument closes the target $0.758$, and it does not claim literature
priority for [Ando's completion theorem](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/14381/1/math_034_011-015.pdf).
The contribution implemented here is
the conversion of the exact planar completion condition into linear
probability witnesses that remain valid on a complete interval box of
unknown input states and terminal-POVM parameters.

The outcome is mixed and informative. The condition detects nonphysical
optimizers missed by separate effect positivity, produces eight rigorous
common-instrument cuts in a 1,000-node cover, and lowers the leading open
bound by $1.4678974560\times10^{-6}$ relative to the equal-budget baseline.
It does not close the target and does not dominate the baseline tree in every
metric. The remaining obstruction is now localized in the convergent
input-cell/McCormick relaxation rather than in an omitted planar
complete-positivity condition.

## 1. Fixed-input completion problem

Let $E_0,E_1,E_2$ be a nondegenerate rank-one ternary POVM spanning the
planar operator system
$$
  \mathcal S=\operatorname{span}\{I,X,Y\}.
$$
For one path outcome $y$, observed terminal statistics determine the pulled
effects
$$
  H_{yt}=\Phi_y^*(E_t).
$$
Write
$$
  F_y=\sum_t H_{yt},\qquad
  (B_{x,y},B_{y,y})=R(A,B)(H_{y0},H_{y1},H_{y2}),
$$
where $R(A,B)$ is the exact planar reconstruction matrix for terminal
parameters $A,B$.

The missing action on $Z$ can be completed to a completely positive qubit
map exactly when the associated partial Choi block has a positive completion.
The Ando numerical-radius criterion implemented in
`planar_cp_completion.py` is equivalent to
$$
  f(F_y)\ge
  \cos\phi\,f(B_{x,y})+\sin\phi\,f(B_{y,y})
  \tag{1}
$$
for every phase $\phi$ and every positive rank-one test functional
$f=(1,n)$, $\lVert n\rVert_2=1$. Equivalently, the maximum of the right side
minus the left side is nonpositive. For fixed $\phi$, maximization over $n$ is
analytic; only a one-dimensional phase search remains.

This condition couples all three terminal outcomes. It is therefore strictly
stronger than requiring each $H_{yt}$ to be positive separately. The test
suite contains an explicit triple of positive effects summing to identity
whose Ando completion radius is $1.72444066\ldots>1$.

## 2. Removing the unknown input inverse

Let the four subnormalized input states have Pauli rows
$$
  R_z=(p_z,r_{z,x},r_{z,y},r_{z,z}),
  \qquad R\in\mathbb R^{4\times4},
$$
and let
$$
  q_{zyt}=R_z\cdot h_{yt}
$$
be the observed probabilities, where $h_{yt}$ is the Pauli vector of
$H_{yt}$. On an input box where $\det R$ has fixed sign $s$, Cramer's
rule gives
$$
  |\det R|\,f(h_{yt})
  =\sum_z w_z(R;f)q_{zyt},
  \tag{2}
$$
with
$$
  w_z(R;f)=s\det R[z\leftarrow f].
$$
The row-replacement determinant is multi-affine in the twelve unreplaced
input coordinates. Its exact minimum and maximum on an axis-aligned box are
therefore attained at the $2^{12}$ vertices. The determinant sign itself is
certified analogously at all $2^{16}$ vertices.

For fixed $f,\phi$, define
$$
  c_t(A,B,\phi)=
  1-\cos\phi\,R_{x,t}(A,B)
   -\sin\phi\,R_{y,t}(A,B).
$$
Multiplying Eq. (1) by $|\det R|$ and using Eq. (2) yields the exact probability
inequality
$$
  G_y(R,A,B;f,\phi)
  =\sum_{z,t}w_z(R;f)c_t(A,B,\phi)q_{zyt}\ge0.
  \tag{3}
$$

## 3. A robust linear cut on a complete box

Let $W_z$ be the outward interval enclosure of $w_z$, and $C_t$ the
outward enclosure of $c_t$, over the complete input and terminal cells.
Define
$$
  U_{zt}=\sup(W_zC_t).
$$
All $q_{zyt}$ are nonnegative, so every physical point in the box obeys
$$
  0\le G_y\le\sum_{z,t}U_{zt}q_{zyt}.
$$
Consequently,
$$
  \boxed{\sum_{z,t}U_{zt}q_{zyt}\ge0}
  \tag{4}
$$
is a valid linear outer cut. If a relaxed optimizer makes the left side
negative, no common completely positive planar instrument exists anywhere in
that box.

The enclosure is outward rounded after every interval operation. The
row-replacement ranges use exhaustive multi-affine vertices rather than
ordinary interval determinant arithmetic. At zero box width, Eq. (4) agrees with
the determinant-scaled exact Ando margin to floating-point rounding error.

## 4. Separation and branching

At a relaxed optimizer, the strongest rank-one direction is generated by
maximizing
$$
  d_0(\phi)+\|d(\phi)\|_2,
  \qquad
  d_\mu(\phi)=
  \cos\phi\,(B_x)_\mu+
  \sin\phi\,(B_y)_\mu-F_\mu.
$$
The maximizing Bloch vector is $n=d/\lVert d\rVert$. This numerical maximization only
selects a direction; validity of the resulting cut follows independently
from Eq. (4).

A direction is called near-certifiable when its interval-enclosure gap is at
most $1.4$ times the magnitude of its exact negative margin. Branching then
uses a two-stage heuristic:

1. midpoint cofactors rank all sixteen input coordinates by first-order
   sensitivity of Eq. (3);
2. only the four strongest coordinates are virtually bisected, and the exact
   exhaustive enclosure is recomputed on both children.

The heuristic changes neither the convex bound nor cut validity. It only
chooses which complete input box to solve next. Ando-margin and
positive-effect determinant-margin splits are limited to one consecutive
generation and alternate with the global McCormick product-residual rule.

## 5. Verification

The implementation is in
`ternary_bilinear_instrument_input_cover.py`. Tests establish four separate
claims:

- a known triple of individually positive pulled effects violates common CP
  completion;
- the zero-width interval expression matches Cramer's rule;
- a tight nonzero box produces a robust Ando cut while all separate-effect
  witnesses remain silent;
- random physical CP maps are never rejected by the outward enclosure.

A fifth test checks that cofactor-prescreened branching retains a split with a
strict exact reduction of the robust enclosure gap.

These tests distinguish mathematical validity from frontier usefulness.
Passing the counterexample test proves that the cut family is genuinely
stronger than separate effect positivity. It does not prove that the family
will dominate every optimizer in the production tree.

## 6. Equal-budget frontier result

Both covers use the same localized top Fourier spectral cell, target
$0.758$, CLARABEL safety $2\times10^{-6}$, and a 1,000-node budget.

| quantity | common-POVM baseline | Ando-strengthened |
|---|---:|---:|
| solved nodes | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| closed nodes | 33 | 30 |
| pending nodes | 935 | 941 |
| maximum pending bound | 0.7633741152384063 | 0.7633726473409502 |
| all determinant-scaled witnesses | 827 | 811 |
| robust Ando witnesses | 0 | 8 |
| maximum depth | 23 | 23 |

Thus
$$
  \Delta_{\rm Ando}
  =0.7633741152384063-0.7633726473409502
  =1.4678974560\times10^{-6}>0.
$$
The improvement is strict but small, and the Ando tree closes fewer nodes.
There is no claim of tree dominance.

Of 970 recorded Ando audits, 925 found an exact fixed-point violation. Only
eight became box-robust cuts at this budget. The branching rule selected 145
Ando-margin splits, 140 separate-effect determinant splits, and 685 product
residual splits.

## 7. Local ablation

At the first robust-witness node (identifier 376), resolving the identical
cell gives the following solver-conditional bounds:

| active cut set | bound |
|---|---:|
| ten inherited cuts | 0.7629220317554689 |
| inherited plus the new Ando cut | 0.7629005135148549 |
| inherited plus two new positive-effect cuts | 0.7628570180562103 |
| all thirteen cuts | 0.7628570195666214 |

The Ando cut alone improves the local bound by about
$2.15\times10^{-5}$, but its effect is numerically subsumed after the two
new positive-effect cuts are also present. This is why the global gain is
much smaller than the existence of exact violations might suggest.

After all cuts at that node, the closest exact Ando margin is
$-0.0026955233$, while its robust upper envelope is
$0.0001123744$. A targeted input split improves the normalized enclosure
margin by $0.05363$; splitting terminal $A$ or $B$ improves it by only
$0.000536$ or $0.000869$. Terminal subdivision is therefore not the
current priority.

## 8. What is established and what remains open

Established:

- separate positivity of the pulled terminal effects does not imply a common
  completely positive instrument;
- the planar Ando condition can be converted into rigorous linear probability
  cuts over nonzero input and terminal boxes;
- those cuts occur on the hard frontier and produce a strict equal-budget
  bound improvement.

Not established:

- the target $0.758$ is not closed;
- the 1,000-node SDP values are not solver-independent interval bounds;
- no novelty claim is made merely for Ando completion or numerical radius;
- the strengthened tree is not uniformly better than the baseline.

The leading bound still lies $0.00537264734$ above target. The data identify
the next bottleneck as the outer convexification of products between a common
input basis and common Choi variables. In the zero-width input limit, the
Ando criterion is exact; therefore adding more scalar planar positivity
conditions without shrinking or strengthening those product envelopes is not
the right next move.

That diagnosis was subsequently confirmed.  Matrix-order conic-RLT
localizers for the scalar--Choi products, combined with the exact lifted
partial-trace identity of one common instrument, close this selected region in
49 nodes at `0.7579083037237451`.  The result and its sandwich-only control are
documented in `common_instrument_product_localizers.md`; the scope remains
localized rather than global.
