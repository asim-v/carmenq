# Determinant-scaled common-POVM witnesses

## Result and status

This note isolates one exact cut for the nondegenerate interior of the
ternary `lambda = 0.55` relaxation.  The cut enforces, over an entire input
box, that all measured statistics could have arisen from one positive qubit
effect.  It is stronger than testing the effect reconstructed only at the
current optimizer and cheaper than solving a new common-instrument
projection at every point.

The result removes a false numerical obstruction.  Ordinary interval
arithmetic says that the determinant of every one of the twenty leading
input boxes may cross zero.  Exhaustive multi-affine vertex evaluation proves
instead that all twenty boxes have strictly positive determinant; the
smallest certified distance from zero is `4.5058889e-05`.  Cramer's rule can
therefore be used on all of them without a singular-case disjunction.

The resulting branch-and-cut calculation does **not** close the frontier.
At a budget of 1,000 solved nodes, the selected cell retains the
solver-conditional upper bound `0.7633741152384063`, above the target
`0.758`.  The output is a valid strengthening and a useful kill result for
this particular scalar strategy, not a proof of the complete interior
frontier.

## Setup

Write four subnormalised qubit inputs and one candidate effect in Pauli
coordinates,

\[
 \rho_z=\frac12\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}R_{z\mu}\sigma_\mu,
 \qquad
 E_k=\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}a_{\mu k}\sigma_\mu .
\]

Their measured probabilities form a column

\[
 q_{zk}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_zE_k),
 \qquad q_k=Ra_k.
\]

For a qubit effect, positivity is the Lorentz-cone condition

\[
 a_{0k}\geq\lVert \boldsymbol a_k\rVert_2.
\]

Equivalently, (f^{\mathsf T}a_k\geq0) for every vector (f) in the
self-dual Lorentz cone.  When a reconstructed effect is nonpositive, the
boundary direction

\[
 f=\left(1,-\frac{\boldsymbol a_k}
 {\lVert\boldsymbol a_k\rVert_2}\right)
\]

exposes its negative eigenvalue.

## Exact determinant-sign enclosure

Let \(\mathcal B=[L,U]\) be a rectangular box of real (4\times4)
matrices.  The determinant is affine in each of its sixteen entries when the
others are held fixed.  A multi-affine function on a rectangle reaches its
minimum and maximum at vertices, hence

\[
 \min_{R\in\mathcal B}\det R
 =\min_{R\in\operatorname{vert}(\mathcal B)}\det R,
 \qquad
 \max_{R\in\mathcal B}\det R
 =\max_{R\in\operatorname{vert}(\mathcal B)}\det R.
\]

Only (2^{16}=65{,}536) evaluations are required.  After fixing one row,
the row-replacement determinant below has twelve free coordinates and needs
only (2^{12}=4{,}096) evaluations.  The implementation accumulates the
Leibniz expansion and adds a conservative floating-point guard.  Thus
“exact” here means exhaustive extrema of the real multi-affine box problem,
with a guarded binary64 enclosure; it does not mean symbolic rational
arithmetic.

## Robust Cramer witness

Assume the determinant has constant sign (s\in\{-1,+1\}) throughout
\(\mathcal B\).  Let (R_{z\leftarrow f}) denote (R) with row (z)
replaced by (f^{\mathsf T}), and define

\[
 w_z(R;f)=s\det(R_{z\leftarrow f}),
 \qquad
 \overline w_z(f)=\max_{R\in\mathcal B}w_z(R;f).
\]

The maxima \(\overline w_z\) are the exhaustive row-replacement vertex
maxima described above.

**Proposition.** If a nonnegative probability column (q_k) is generated by
some (R\in\mathcal B) and some positive qubit effect (a_k), then

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{z=0}^{3}\overline w_z(f)q_{zk}\geq0}
\]

for every Lorentz-cone direction (f).

**Proof.** Cramer's rule and expansion along the replaced row give

\[
 \sum_z w_z(R;f)q_{zk}
 =s f^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{adj}(R)q_k
 =|\det R|f^{\mathsf T}a_k\geq0.
\]

Because each (q_{zk}\geq0) and
(w_z(R;f)\leq\overline w_z(f)), replacing every coefficient by its box
maximum can only increase the left-hand side.  Therefore the boxed
inequality is necessary for every (R\) in the complete box.  A strict
negative value rejects the box, not merely the point at which (f) was
chosen.  \(\square\)

The direction (f) may be generated from a relaxed optimizer: validity does
not depend on that optimizer being physical.  Once generated, the direction
and all four coefficient bounds are fixed numerical data.  The cut is linear
in the probability variables and can be inherited by every descendant input
cell.

## Why this is a common-instrument cut

For an instrument \(\{\Phi_y\}\) followed by terminal POVM
\(\{E_t\}\), the pulled-back operators

\[
 F_{yt}=\Phi_y^\dagger(E_t)
\]

are positive and sum to the identity.  The twelve pairs (k=(y,t)) must
therefore form one effective POVM on the four inputs.  Violation of any
determinant-scaled inequality rules out both that common POVM and every
common instrument that could induce it.  The converse does not hold: a
common effective POVM need not factor through the required instrument and
terminal measurement.  The witness is consequently a necessary cut, not a
complete common-instrument criterion.

This relationship is consistent with finite-state interpolation and quantum
comparison theory.  Ambrozie and Gheondea formulate completely positive
matrix interpolation with additional restrictions in [arXiv:1308.0667](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0667).
Buscemi's [comparison of quantum statistical models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3794)
and Jenčová's [comparison of quantum channels and statistical
experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06370) place the existence of a
common transformation in a broader statistical-comparison framework.  The
use of McCormick envelopes and spatial refinement is standard global
optimisation technology; quantitative limitations of the bilinear envelope
are analysed by Boland *et al.* in [arXiv:1507.08703](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08703).

The Cramer identity, Lorentz self-duality, and vertex-extremum fact are not
claimed as new mathematics.  The project contribution is their integration
as inherited, box-valid common-POVM cuts in this particular temporal-memory
frontier, together with a reproducible diagnosis of where those cuts cease
to be decisive.

## Numerical experiment

The run combines one shared positive Choi family, one shared effective POVM,
all input--Choi and input--effect products with McCormick envelopes,
pure-prefix spherical caps, interval-safe terminal effects, and the robust
cuts above.  A hybrid branching policy permits at most one consecutive
determinant-margin split and otherwise follows the largest product residual.
This prevents a single almost-robust effect from starving convergence of the
other bilinear products.

| quantity | result |
|---|---:|
| localised starting bound | `0.7635145905297471` |
| target | `0.758` |
| solved / closed / pending nodes | `1000 / 33 / 935` |
| unresolved solver nodes | `0` |
| generated robust witnesses | `827` |
| nodes adding at least one witness | `310` |
| determinant / product-residual branches | `223 / 744` |
| maximum depth | `23` |
| final maximum pending bound | `0.7633741152384063` |
| fraction of the initial target gap removed | `2.547%` |

The exact determinant audit was active at 924 solved nodes, and 59 nodes
produced at least one robustly violated direction.  All twenty leading open
cells are sign-positive by exhaustive vertex enclosure although ordinary
interval arithmetic crosses zero in all twenty.  The leading cell illustrates
the dependency gap:

\[
 \det R\in[-0.00490471,0.02176919]
 \quad\text{(ordinary interval arithmetic)},
\]

whereas exhaustive vertex evaluation gives

\[
 \det R\in[0.000849877,0.017490875].
\]

The full checkpoint is intentionally not committed because it is about
23 MB and contains every solver vector.  Its SHA-256 digest, runtime, tree
accounting, and the independently recomputed leading-cell enclosures are in
[`determinant_povm_cover_l055_summary.json`](../scratch/d2_frontier/determinant_povm_cover_l055_summary.json).

## Kill result and common-instrument follow-up

The scalar common-POVM witnesses are not the missing global certificate.
The best remaining cells reconstruct nine or ten nonpositive effective
effects at their relaxed optima, but their boxes are still wide enough that
the coefficient-wise Cramer maxima erase the violation.  Blindly adding more
directions cannot fix that enclosure loss, and determinant-only branching
was empirically worse because it neglected the rest of the bilinear
relaxation.

The follow-up asks for the matrix coupling of a common instrument. For a
nonsingular input basis, the unique map for outcome \(y\) has Choi numerator

\[
 K_y=\frac12\sum_{\mu,\nu}
 \left[Q_y^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{adj}(R^{\mathsf T})\right]_{\nu\mu}
 \sigma_\mu^{\mathsf T}\otimes\sigma_\nu,
 \qquad sK_y\succeq0.
\]

For the planar terminal operator system, Ando's theorem converts existence of
the missing output coordinate into a coupled family of support-function
inequalities. This retains the exact fixed-input completion condition without
explicitly carrying that coordinate, correcting the stronger requirement
stated in the first version of this note.

The completed follow-up multiplies those inequalities by \(|\det R|\) and
interval-encloses their Cramer coefficients over complete input and terminal
boxes. In the equal-budget 1,000-node comparison it generates eight robust
common-instrument cuts and gives a strict but small \(1.47\times10^{-6}\)
leading-bound improvement; it does not close the target. Details and the
compact audit are in
[`determinant_scaled_ando_instrument_witnesses.md`](determinant_scaled_ando_instrument_witnesses.md).

## Reproduction

From `scratch/d2_frontier`, with the optional frontier dependencies installed:

```bash
python ternary_bilinear_instrument_input_cover.py \
  --frontier-json ternary_reconstructed_depth4_g2_top_leaf_bbb_p1_s92_l055.json \
  --output ternary_exactdet_hybrid_instrument_topcell_pilot_l055.json \
  --top-spectral-cell --max-nodes 1000 --checkpoint-every 20

python summarize_determinant_povm_cover.py \
  ternary_exactdet_hybrid_instrument_topcell_pilot_l055.json \
  --output determinant_povm_cover_l055_summary.json
```

The convex bounds use CLARABEL and remain solver-conditional.  The summary
validator checks the complete binary-tree accounting and recomputes the
leading determinant enclosures, but it is not a solver-independent proof of
the reported objective bound.
