\section{Discussion}

The benchmark is useful because it turns an imprecise question about reversible records into one number with an exact null. A high AUDIT score alone may come from destructive classical measurement. A high RETURN score alone may come from doing nothing. Requiring both after a hidden late choice makes persistent coherence useful, while immediate sequestration prevents a classical device from compiling the global parity into one collective measurement.

The first-order transition is a structural consequence of asking for a parity of many fresh events. Classical local evidence combines multiplicatively: a modest per-slot record becomes exponentially weak as a parity predictor. Global return fidelity also multiplies. For $n\geq3$, the supported optimum therefore avoids intermediate strategies over a range of weights and jumps from no measurement to almost projective measurement. The phenomenon is a decision transition in the optimal null, not a thermodynamic phase transition in matter.

The strict gap between streamed and collective classical recording is equally important. It demonstrates why a temporal access model can reveal a resource separation that a one-shot information--disturbance curve cannot. The coherent accumulator then shows that the task does not demand a growing quantum memory: one protected temporal qubit is sufficient for an arbitrarily long parity stream in the noiseless circuit. Experimentally, of course, global return becomes increasingly fragile with $n$.

\subsection{Limitations}

The benchmark is not device independent. It trusts the EPR source, the branch measurements, the sequestration station, the timing boundary, and the absence or calibrated allowance of uncharged coherent side channels. It certifies incompatibility with a declared classical-memory comb; it does not reconstruct the device's internal dynamics or prove that the named qubit was the only memory.

Visible terminal reset is not global erasure. A classical controller may reset its public port while dumping its transcript into an inaccessible environment. The present null allows this and the theorem remains valid. Demanding decoupling from every purification would define a different, stronger task.

The finite-statistics result assumes independent Bernoulli trials and fixed sample sizes. Drift, correlated errors, adaptive stopping, multiple lengths, and estimated systematic bounds need dedicated inference. The phenomenological forecast is a planning example, not a hardware claim.

No interpretation of quantum mechanics is selected. A successful result says that a coherent temporal resource outperforms an explicit classical-memory model on an ordinary quantum-information task.

\subsection{Extensions}

Natural next problems are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item exact frontiers for $q$-ary streams and predicates beyond parity;
  \item tight bounds for a finite coherent memory dimension between zero and one full logical qubit per task;
  \item robust nulls with bounded cross-slot leakage rather than a sharp classical/quantum dichotomy;
  \item sequentially valid inference for random stopping and adaptive length selection;
  \item semi-device-independent or device-independent variants with operationally certified sequestration;
  \item hardware experiments that compare streamed and collective access using the same physical components.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusion}

For a specific streamed parity task, unlimited adaptive classical memory obeys an exact audit--return frontier, collective classical processing performs strictly better, and one coherent temporal qubit reaches the algebraic optimum. This is a compact, falsifiable target for quantum-memory experiments. The main theoretical work is complete under the declared null; the main experimental work is to make that null physically credible.
