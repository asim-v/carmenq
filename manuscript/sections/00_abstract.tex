\begin{abstract}
We study whether a controlled quantum process can contain a nontrivial internal history, including memory and conditional action, while retaining only a selected predicate as an interferometric phase after all branch-distinguishing records are uncomputed. The elementary circuit is physically valid but not, by itself, evidence for an internal history: a known closed sequence $U_H^\dagger O_p U_H$ is endpoint-equivalent to a directly compiled effective unitary. We make this limitation central. We define a zero-record condition as the absence of residual information that distinguishes histories within each allowed predicate class, formulate reset, leakage, visibility, completeness, and soundness criteria, and separate closed-circuit validation from causal certification under restricted temporal access. We prove phase survival, endpoint compression, and no-transcription statements; show why reset fidelity alone cannot certify recoherence; and formulate an analytic late-challenge reversible-memory benchmark. A density-matrix reference implementation reproduces the exact visibility--environment-overlap relation, compares coherent, incoherent, retained-memory, and direct-phase controls, and quantifies dephasing, amplitude damping, depolarisation, environmental leakage, inversion error, depth scaling, and coarse-grained marginal transcript leakage. The results establish a reproducible baseline and a set of kill criteria. They do not establish a device-independent causal witness, an experimental late-challenge score, cryptographic zero knowledge, a quantum advantage, objective collapse, or a preferred interpretation of quantum mechanics. The open scientific target is a resource-bounded separation that certifies causal memory while preserving zero-record recoherence.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} reversible quantum computation; multitime quantum processes; causal memory; interferometry; uncomputation; quantum records; recoherence.
