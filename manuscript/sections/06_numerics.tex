\section{Reference simulations}
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Implementation}

The simulator evolves an explicit \(256\times256\) density matrix on
\begin{equation}
\Hil_B(4)\otimes\Hil_W(4)\otimes\Hil_M(4)
\otimes\Hil_G(2)\otimes\Hil_A(2).
\end{equation}
No quantum-software development kit or circuit optimiser is used. Reversible basis transformations are compiled as permutations; local channels are applied by Kraus contraction; reduced states are obtained by explicit partial traces. The four history labels encode two logical bits, and the allowed predicate is parity,
\begin{equation}
p=(0,1,1,0).
\end{equation}
Thus each predicate fibre contains two histories and can hide one bit.

The default history first adds \(B\) into \(W\) modulo four and copies \(W\) into \(M\). In round \(r\), after memory formation in the gate sequence, a configured challenge parameter \(c_r\) is applied. The simulator does not model an independent challenge source, conceal \(c_r\) from earlier gates, or score the ZR-3 random-access game. The transducer computes
\begin{equation}
d_r=m_{r\bmod2}\oplus c_r
\end{equation}
into \(G\), conditionally flips a world bit, and uncomputes \(G\). The predicate oracle reads the final internal state:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{int}}
=\operatorname{parity}(W)\oplus\bigoplus_r d_r
=\operatorname{parity}(B)
\end{equation}
on the intended path. It never reads \(B\) directly. The phase ancilla starts in \(\ket-\), so a controlled bit flip writes \((-1)^{p_{\mathrm{int}}}\). The history is then inverted and \(H\otimes H\) maps the parity phase pattern to \(\ket{11}_B\).

The metrics are normalised multipath \(l_1\) coherence
\begin{equation}
V_{l_1}=\frac1{K-1}\sum_{b\neq b'}|(\rho_B)_{bb'}|,
\qquad K=4,
\end{equation}
reset population
\begin{equation}
\Freset=\Pr[W=0,M=0,G=0],
\end{equation}
predicate fidelity \(F_p=\Pr[B=3]\) after readout, and conditional Holevo information \(\chi(H:WMG|P)\). The first quantity is a normalised multipath coherence measure; it is not interchangeable with every two-path fringe convention.

\subsection{Environmental records}

The environment model assigns pure records with constant pairwise overlap \(\eta\). Tracing out the environment multiplies every off-diagonal branch block by \(\eta\). The simulation reproduces the analytic laws to floating-point precision:
\begin{equation}
V_{l_1}=\eta,
\qquad
F_p=\frac14+\frac34\eta,
\qquad
\chi(H:E|P)=h_2\!\left(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
At \(\eta=0.5\), the corresponding values are \(0.5\), \(0.625\), and \(0.811278\) bits. Reset remains exactly one throughout the sweep, directly demonstrating that local reset is not recoherence.

\subsection{Comparative Markovian channels}

After each addressed logical operation, the simulator can apply qudit dephasing, full qudit depolarisation, or relaxation of every excited level toward zero. These are explicit comparative channels, not calibrations for a physical processor. In particular, four-level relaxation is not identical to independent amplitude damping on two hardware qubits.

Figure~\ref{fig:noise} shows that the metrics respond differently. Computational-basis dephasing leaves reset populations unchanged while reducing coherence and predicate fidelity. Depolarisation reduces all three. Relaxation can favour the zero reset subspace while still degrading the logical phase. This is why a single scalar fidelity cannot characterise the protocol.

\subsection{Inverse reliability and depth}

Imperfect inversion is modelled as a stochastic skipped inverse:
\begin{equation}
\Chan_{\mathrm{inv}}(\rho)
=(1-\epsilon)U^\dagger\rho U+\epsilon\rho.
\end{equation}
At \(\epsilon=0.03\) per inverse operation, visibility is \(0.825798\), reset fidelity \(0.874200\), predicate fidelity \(0.869349\), and the controlled records retain \(0.113596\) bits within parity classes. The model is intentionally simple and must not be confused with coherent over-rotation or leakage outside the logical Hilbert space.

The depth sweep increases challenge-responsive rounds from one to eight, corresponding to 11 through 53 logical operations, with per-addressed-operation dephasing \(0.003\) and depolarisation \(0.001\). Every ideal circuit remains exact. At 53 operations the noisy simulation gives
\begin{equation}
V_{l_1}=0.892778,\qquad
\Freset=0.969516,\qquad
F_p=0.919584.
\end{equation}
The numerical curve quantifies this model only; it is not a universal observer-complexity law.

\subsection{Numerical claims and their boundary}

Fourteen automated tests check permutation bijectivity, density-matrix invariants, trace preservation, Kraus completeness, exact ideal reset, classical-mixture loss, retained-record leakage, direct-phase equivalence, action dependence, the analytic overlap law, monotonic selected noise sweeps, and byte-for-byte reproducibility of generated tables under a fixed seed. These tests validate the implemented artifact. They do not test an independent late-challenge source, establish that a black-box device executed an irreducible history, certify joint leakage from correlated marginal records, or provide a quantum computational advantage.
